Pricing Justification

Side-by-side cost comparison, 3-year total cost of ownership, and rationale for per-organization pricing. Designed for the finance committee and procurement review package.

Bottom line

A typical Pro subscription pays for itself in under 8 weeks.

A mid-size city processing 60 meetings per year spends roughly $42,000 annually on clerk-led manual transcription and summarization. CivicLens Pro delivers the same outputs plus automated extraction, natural-language Q&A, and FOIA-ready export for $9,588 per year. Net annual savings: roughly $32,000.

Why per-organization, not per-seat?

Most SaaS vendors price by the seat. CivicLens does not, and the reason is mission-aligned.

Civic transparency tools work best when every staff member, council member, intern, press liaison, and community engagement officer who needs access can get it. If we charged per seat, we would be creating friction against our own value proposition and rewarding buyers for keeping information siloed.

Instead, CivicLens prices per organization (tenant). Plans are scoped by query volume, which scales with real usage rather than with the number of people on your team roster. Every plan allows unlimited named users within the tenant.

What this means for procurement: no per-seat math, no true-up invoices at year end, no "we need to add three more people" change orders. The line item on your contract stays flat.

Benefits beyond the dollar savings

Faster FOIA response

Public-records requests that used to require a clerk to manually scan hours of video now resolve in minutes. FOIA-compliant exports are built into the platform.

Better public engagement

Residents, press, and watchdog groups get a searchable front door to city decisions. Lower friction on information access reduces repeat citizen questions to the clerk's office.

Institutional memory

When a long-serving clerk retires, their institutional knowledge doesn't walk out the door. The archive remains searchable and queryable by any successor.

Defensible audit trail

Every question asked, every answer given, every citation — all logged to an append-only audit database with SHA-256 integrity hashes. This matters for SOC 2, FISMA, and public-records law.
