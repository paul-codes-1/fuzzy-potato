The Challenge

The Valley Dispatch is a regional news outlet covering five cities in the Sacramento metropolitan area. Like many local outlets, they have seen their newsroom shrink over the past decade. Today, one reporter covers the government beat across all five cities — roughly 15-20 public meetings per month, each averaging 90 minutes of recorded video.

The math simply did not work:

25-30 hrs

Monthly hours needed to watch all meeting recordings — more than half the reporter's available working time for the beat

3-4 FOIAs

Filed per week for basic meeting information like vote tallies, contract details, and speaker names — each taking days to weeks for response

2 of 5

Cities receiving meaningful coverage in any given week. The other three were essentially unmonitored unless a tip came in.

Scooped

Regularly beaten to stories by competing outlets or social media when significant decisions happened in the unmonitored cities

"I'd spend my Monday watching Friday's council meeting from City A, and while I'm doing that, City B and City C are having their meetings. I was always behind. And the stories I missed weren't minor — we're talking contract awards, zoning changes, budget amendments. The public deserves coverage of those decisions."

The Solution

The Dispatch deployed CivicLens Professional to monitor all five cities' Granicus instances. The reporter configured the system around three primary workflows:

The journalist's CivicLens workflow

1

Morning Scan: AI-Generated Meeting Digests

Each morning, the reporter reviews CivicLens summaries of the previous evening's meetings across all five cities. A 90-minute meeting distills to a 3-minute read. Key items — controversial votes, large contracts, public outcry — are immediately identifiable from the extracted facts.

2

Investigative Research: Natural Language Q&A

For deeper stories, the reporter asks questions across the entire archive: "How much has City B spent on outside legal counsel in the last 12 months?" or "Which council members have spoken against the proposed development on Elm Street?" Answers come with timestamped citations linking to the exact moment in the original video.

3

Financial Tracking: Dollar Amount Alerts

CivicLens automatically extracts every dollar amount, contract award, and budget amendment. The reporter set up alerts for any financial item above $500K. This has been the single most productive story-generation feature — surfacing newsworthy spending decisions that would otherwise be buried in committee meetings.

4

Verification: Click-to-Video Timestamps

Every claim in CivicLens is linked to a timestamp in the original Granicus video. Before publishing, the reporter clicks through to verify quotes and context directly in the source material. No more "the AI said so" — every fact is verifiable in seconds.

The reporter went from 4-5 government stories per month to 12-15. The increase came not from working longer hours, but from CivicLens surfacing newsworthy items across all five cities that previously went undetected. Every story was still fact-checked against original sources.

Stories Powered by CivicLens

Exclusive

$1.8M Emergency Water Main Contract Awarded Without Public Bid

CivicLens financial alert flagged a $1.8M emergency contract award buried in a consent agenda. The Q&A system revealed the same vendor had received three previous emergency contracts totaling $4.2M over 18 months. The story led to a council review of emergency procurement policies.

Investigation

Council Member Reversed Position on Housing Development After Developer Donation

Using the Q&A system to track a council member's statements about a controversial housing project across 8 months of meetings, the reporter documented a clear shift in position between first reading and final vote. Paired with campaign finance records, the story generated significant reader engagement.

Public Interest

Three Cities Quietly Cut Parks Maintenance Budgets by Combined $2.1M

CivicLens financial tracking across all five cities revealed that three had reduced parks maintenance budgets during mid-year amendments. No single city's cut was large enough to generate news on its own, but the regional pattern told a story about municipal budget priorities that resonated with readers.
