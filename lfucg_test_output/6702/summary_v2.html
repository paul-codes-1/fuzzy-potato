Highway Safety Targets for 2026

[timestamp: 60:00] The Committee discussed and approved highway safety targets for 2026 as part of the city's ongoing commitment to improving road safety. The discussion focused on establishing specific metrics for reducing traffic fatalities and serious injuries, with the ultimate goal of achieving zero traffic deaths by 2050.

Mr. Aviglia presented the proposed safety targets, outlining the framework for measuring progress in reducing highway-related incidents throughout the city. The targets are designed to align with broader regional and state safety initiatives while addressing local traffic safety concerns.

Council Member Baxter participated in the discussion, contributing to the evaluation of the proposed metrics and their feasibility for implementation over the coming year. The conversation addressed how these targets would be monitored and what specific measures would be taken to achieve the established goals.

The safety targets represent part of a comprehensive approach to traffic safety that extends beyond 2026, with the ambitious long-term objective of eliminating all traffic fatalities by 2050. This "Vision Zero" approach requires setting incremental annual targets that demonstrate measurable progress toward the ultimate goal.

The Committee approved the highway safety targets for 2026, formally adopting the metrics that will guide the city's traffic safety efforts throughout the year. The approval establishes the framework for tracking progress and implementing safety improvements across the city's transportation network.

This resolution reflects the city's commitment to data-driven safety improvements and provides clear benchmarks for evaluating the effectiveness of traffic safety programs and infrastructure investments planned for 2026.

Lexington Regional Transit Asset Condition Targets for 2026

[timestamp: 150:00] The Committee reviewed and approved asset condition targets for Lexington Regional Transit's fleet and facilities for 2026. Mr. Aviglia and Fred presented the discussion on the current state of transit assets and established performance benchmarks for the upcoming year.

The presentation covered both revenue and non-revenue vehicles in the transit system's fleet, examining their operational condition and maintenance requirements. The speakers also addressed facility conditions as part of the comprehensive asset management review.

The asset condition targets are designed to ensure the transit system maintains reliable service while managing the lifecycle of its equipment and infrastructure. These targets serve as performance metrics for evaluating the effectiveness of maintenance programs and capital investment decisions.

The Committee approved the proposed 2026 asset condition targets as presented, establishing clear benchmarks for the transit system's asset management program for the coming year.

Federal Transportation Reauthorization

[timestamp: 300:00] The committee received an informational briefing on upcoming changes to federal transportation legislation. Mr. Aviglia led the discussion regarding the expiration of the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) and the introduction of new federal legislation known as the Basics Act.

The presentation focused on how these legislative changes would impact federal transportation funding streams and planning requirements at the local level. Mr. Aviglia outlined the key differences between the current IIJA framework and the proposed Basics Act, highlighting potential changes to funding allocation methods and planning processes that municipalities and transportation agencies would need to navigate.

The discussion was informational in nature, with no formal action taken by the committee. The briefing served to prepare committee members for the transition period as federal transportation policy evolves and to ensure awareness of how these changes might affect local transportation projects and funding opportunities.

This agenda item provided committee members with essential background information on the federal legislative landscape affecting transportation infrastructure, allowing them to better understand the broader policy context that influences local transportation planning and project implementation.
