## Votes and Decisions

The Council took several votes during the August 14, 2007 meeting, with most items passing by voice vote.

**Rezoning Public Hearing** [timestamp: 10:00]
CM Moloney moved to place a public hearing on the docket for October 2, 2007 regarding rezoning of 1388 Alexandria Drive, seconded by CM Stinnett. The motion passed by voice vote.

**Meeting Summary Approval** [timestamp: 20:00]
CM Gorton moved to approve the July 10, 2007 meeting summary, seconded by CM Crosbie. The motion passed by voice vote.

**Budget Amendments** [timestamp: 25:00]
CM Gorton moved to approve budget amendments, seconded by CM Ellinger. The motion passed by voice vote.

**New Business Items** [timestamp: 30:00]
CM Stinnett moved to approve new business items A through JJ, with the exception of item S, seconded by CM Gorton. The motion passed by voice vote.

**Property Tax Rates** [timestamp: 45:00]
CM Lane moved to maintain the prior year's property tax rates with the exception of refuse collection rates, seconded by CM Myers. The motion passed by roll call vote.

**Discretionary Time Motion** [timestamp: 2:30:00]
Vice Mayor Gray moved to table a motion regarding adding four hours of discretionary time. The tabling motion passed by roll call vote with 9 ayes and 6 nays.

All motions that came to a vote were approved, with the Council demonstrating broad consensus on most agenda items through voice votes, while using roll call votes for more significant matters such as tax rates and the discretionary time issue.

## Budget and Financial Actions

The Council approved two significant payments related to Fourth of July celebrations during the August 14, 2007 meeting.

**Resolution 399-07** authorized a payment of $35,000 to Zambelli Fireworks Internationale for the 4th of July Fireworks Display. This payment covered the costs associated with the city's Independence Day fireworks celebration.

**Resolution 400-07** approved a payment of $21,750 to the Lexington Philharmonic Society for the Patriotic Concert. This payment was for the musical performance that accompanied the Fourth of July festivities.

Both resolutions represented expenditures totaling $56,750 for the city's Independence Day programming, covering both the fireworks display and musical entertainment provided by the Lexington Philharmonic Society.

## Contested Items

The August 14, 2007 Council meeting featured two primary areas of contention that generated significant discussion and community concern.

**Employee Picnic and Half-Day Off**

A heated discussion emerged regarding the cancellation of the annual employee picnic and associated half-day off for city workers. Council members debated the decision's impact on employee morale, with some expressing concern that eliminating these benefits could negatively affect city staff motivation and job satisfaction. The discussion centered on whether the cost savings from canceling these employee benefits justified the potential harm to workplace culture and employee relations.

**Meadowbrook Golf Course**

The proposed sale of Meadowbrook Golf Course drew significant community opposition, particularly from Fourth District residents. Citizens and council members raised concerns about the loss of green space in the district, arguing that selling the golf course would eliminate valuable recreational land and reduce the area's environmental benefits. The opposition focused on preserving the property as public green space rather than allowing it to be developed for other purposes.

Both contested items reflected broader tensions between fiscal responsibility and maintaining community amenities and employee benefits. The employee picnic debate highlighted concerns about city worker morale during what appeared to be a period of budget constraints, while the Meadowbrook Golf Course discussion demonstrated community resistance to the potential loss of recreational and environmental resources in favor of revenue generation through property sales.

These contentious discussions required council members to balance competing priorities: maintaining employee satisfaction and community green space against the city's financial needs and budget pressures.
