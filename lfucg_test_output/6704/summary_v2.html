Votes and Decisions

The Council took action on two items during the February 26, 2026 meeting, with both measures passing unanimously.

Ordinance 0130-26 [timestamp: 1:00:00]

Mr. Sevigny motioned to approve An Ordinance amending certain of the Budgets of the Lexington-Fayette Urban County Government to reflect current requirements for municipal expenditures, and appropriating and re-appropriating funds, FY 2026 Schedule No. 23. Ms. Curtis seconded the motion. The ordinance passed by roll call vote with 15 ayes and 0 nays. All Council members voted in favor: Brown, Curtis, Eblen, Ellinger, Elliott Baxter, Hale, Higgins-Hord, Lynch, Morton, Reynolds, Sevigny, Sheehan, Wu, Beasley, and Boone.

Resolution 0051-26 [timestamp: 1:30:00]

Ms. Reynolds motioned to approve A Resolution authorizing the Mayor to execute an Agreement with the Fayette County Board of Education for space to host Truck-A-Palooza. Ms. Higgins-Hord seconded the motion. The resolution passed by roll call vote with 15 ayes and 0 nays. All Council members voted in favor: Brown, Curtis, Eblen, Ellinger, Elliott Baxter, Hale, Higgins-Hord, Lynch, Morton, Reynolds, Sevigny, Sheehan, Wu, Beasley, and Boone.

Both votes demonstrated unanimous Council support, with all 15 members present and voting in favor of each measure. No abstentions were recorded for either item.

Budget and Financial Actions

The Council approved two significant financial expenditures during the February 26, 2026 meeting.

Infrastructure Contract Award

The Council authorized a contract with Lagco, Inc. for $474,477 to construct the East Vine Street Retaining Wall through Resolution 0073-26. This infrastructure project represents a substantial investment in the city's roadway stability and safety improvements.

Recreation Equipment Purchase

Resolution 0067-26 approved the purchase of exercise equipment from Design2Wellness for $175,576.45. This acquisition will enhance the city's recreational facilities and provide improved fitness amenities for residents.

The combined financial commitments totaled $650,053.45 across these two resolutions, covering both essential infrastructure maintenance and community wellness investments.
